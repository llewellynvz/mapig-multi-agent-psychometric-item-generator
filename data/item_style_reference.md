# Item Style Reference — What Good Psychometric Items Sound Like

This file contains real items from established, well-validated psychometric instruments.
These are STYLE examples only. Do NOT copy these items. Use them to calibrate your understanding
of how natural, clear, and human-sounding self-report items should read.

## Key Principles Demonstrated

### 1. Natural Language — Items sound like something a person would actually say

**Satisfaction With Life Scale (SWLS)** — Diener et al. (1985), α = .87
- "In most ways my life is close to my ideal."
- "The conditions of my life are excellent."
- "I am satisfied with my life."
- "So far I have gotten the important things I want in life."
- "If I could live my life over, I would change almost nothing."

Notice: simple vocabulary, first-person, feels personal and introspective — not corporate or clinical.

### 2. Varied Sentence Structure — Not every item follows the same template

**Flourishing Scale (FS)** — Diener et al. (2010), α = .87
- "I lead a purposeful and meaningful life"
- "My social relationships are supportive and rewarding"
- "I am engaged and interested in my daily activities"
- "I actively contribute to the happiness and well-being of others"
- "I am competent and capable in the activities that are important to me"
- "I am a good person and live a good life"
- "I am optimistic about my future"
- "People respect me"

Notice: some start with "I am...", some with "My...", some with "People...", one uses "I lead...", one uses "I actively contribute...". Each item has a distinct angle.

### 3. Concrete Behavioral Referents — Observable thoughts, feelings, or behaviors

**WHO-5 Well-Being Index** — WHO (1998), α = .84
- "I have felt cheerful and in good spirits"
- "I have felt calm and relaxed"
- "I have felt active and vigorous"
- "I woke up feeling fresh and rested"
- "My daily life has been filled with things that interest me"

Notice: specific emotional states ("cheerful", "calm", "vigorous"), concrete experiences ("woke up feeling fresh"), observable outcomes ("filled with things that interest me").

### 4. Emotional Authenticity — Items capture genuine human experience

**GAD-7** — Spitzer et al. (2006), α = .92
- "Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge"
- "Not being able to stop or control worrying"
- "Worrying too much about different things"
- "Trouble relaxing"
- "Being so restless that it's hard to sit still"
- "Becoming easily annoyed or irritable"
- "Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen"

Notice: experiential language ("not being able to stop"), vivid description ("so restless that it's hard to sit still"), emotional honesty ("feeling afraid as if something awful might happen").

### 5. Clinical Precision Without Jargon — Technical accuracy in plain language

**PHQ-9** — Kroenke et al. (2001), α = .89
- "Little interest or pleasure in doing things"
- "Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless"
- "Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much"
- "Feeling tired or having little energy"
- "Poor appetite or overeating"
- "Feeling bad about yourself — or that you are a failure or have let yourself or your family down"
- "Trouble concentrating on things, such as reading the newspaper or watching television"

Notice: anchored with real-world examples ("reading the newspaper or watching television"), natural qualifiers ("little energy", "poor appetite"), no clinical jargon despite measuring a clinical construct.

## Anti-Patterns — What Bad Items Look Like

These are examples of AI-generated items that FAIL the naturalness test:

BAD: "I consider different solutions when my first idea does not work."
- Template: "I [verb] [object] when [condition]" — robotic and formulaic
- "does not work" — stiff; a person would say "doesn't work" or "doesn't pan out"

BAD: "I explore new approaches to understand work tasks."
- Vague: what does "explore" mean concretely? What are "new approaches"?
- "work tasks" — corporate jargon, not how people talk about their jobs

BAD: "I use different methods when I face new situations."
- Generic: could apply to literally any construct (problem-solving, adaptability, creativity)
- Lacks the specificity that makes items discriminate between constructs

BAD: "I update my plans when I receive new information."
- Template repetition: same "I [verb] when [condition]" as every other item
- "receive new information" — formal; a person would say "learn something new" or "find out things have changed"

## How to Use This Reference

When generating items:
1. Read these examples to calibrate your sense of quality
2. Ask: "Would a real person say this naturally?" — if no, rewrite
3. Check: "Does each item have a unique angle?" — if all items follow the same template, diversify
4. Verify: "Is this specific enough to discriminate?" — if the item could measure any construct, sharpen it
5. NEVER copy these items — use them as style models only
